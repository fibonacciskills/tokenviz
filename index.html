For text, the loop is the whole show. For images and video, the loop is replaced by an iterative denoiser that refines a whole grid of tokens or latents at once — but the input and output formats are identical.

Text: a token is a chunk of words.

autoregressive · 1 token ≈ 4 chars

A language model has one job, repeated thousands of times: given the tokens so far, output a probability for every token in the vocabulary — then pick one, append it, and go again. That’s why text streams out word-ish-piece at a time.

The token
A sub-word fragment from a fixed vocabulary (Llama 3: 128k entries; Qwen: ~152k). Common words are one token; rare words get split: lighthouse → light + house.
The loop
Each step reads the entire context and emits one token. Sampling settings — temperature, top-p — decide how adventurous the pick is. The context grows by one token per step.

Image: a token is a patch of meaning.

diffusion · whole grid at once

An image model doesn’t write one pixel at a time. It starts with a grid of pure noise — latents — and scrubs noise out of every cell simultaneously across a few dozen steps, guided by the encoded prompt. Watch the two halves below: the latent grid settling on the left, the decode emerging on the right.

The token
A latent patch — a compressed spatial cell. A 1024×1024 image at 8× compression is a 128 × 128 grid: ~16k patches. Some models (Emu3, Janus, Chameleon) go further and quantize patches into discrete tokens, making images truly part of the vocabulary.
The loop
No next-token loop: a denoiser predicts the noise in all patches at once, subtracts a slice of it, and repeats — FLUX.1 [dev] runs about 28 steps. The prompt enters as text-encoder embeddings that steer every step.

Video: a token is a patch of spacetime.

3D tokens · time × height × width

A video model cuts the clip into small cubes that span several frames at once — so a token carries motion, not just appearance. Token counts explode: seconds of video mean hundreds of thousands of spacetime patches, which is why video came last and why it eats GPU memory for breakfast.

The token
A spacetime patch: a cube spanning a few pixels × a few pixels × a few frames. A 3D VAE compresses the clip first (HunyuanVideo), or frames are patchified directly (Sora’s recipe). One 5-second clip ≈ hundreds of thousands of tokens.
The loop
Same denoising idea as images, but the noise lives in a 3D volume — so neighboring frames are generated together, and consistency across time is enforced by the patch shape itself. That’s the trick that made video coherent.

Open models let you watch the machine.

Weights, tokenizer, sampling loop — all inspectable. You can count your own tokens before you run anything, run a prompt ten times to see the sampling variance, and read the license to know what you’re allowed to build.

Commercial models meter the machine.

Every token, patch, and frame crosses a pricing endpoint. You get the frontier’s best quality and zero ops burden — and in exchange, no view inside, no fixed cost, and your prompts live on someone else’s server.

The token is the unit of trust.

Whoever holds the weights holds the meter. Open models move generation to where your data already lives — a laptop, a lab, an air-gapped box — which is exactly why local-first tooling keeps choosing open models.
