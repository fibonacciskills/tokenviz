Why the count matters

Context windows, latency, and price are all measured in tokens, not words. GPT‑5.2 carries a 400,000-token context and bills input and output at different rates, so a prompt that reads as one sentence and an answer that reads as one page are priced on the same ruler.

Images sent into a model are counted differently again: they are cut into 32 × 32 pixel patches, and the patch count is the token count.

A still image is just a video with one frame, which is why the same patch scheme covers both. The through-line

  • A token is whatever unit a model was trained to predict. For text it's a fragment of a word; for images it's a patch of the frame; for video it's a patch followed across time.
  • Prediction order is the real difference. Text and GPT Image commit left to right. Diffusion models — DALL·E 3, and Sora as a diffusion transformer — refine the whole output at once across steps.
  • The tokenizer is why models miscount letters, stumble on rare names, and charge more for some languages than others: they never see the characters you typed, only the pieces the vocabulary offers.
  • Cost and context are always denominated in these units, which is why "how long is my prompt" is a question about tokens rather than words.
